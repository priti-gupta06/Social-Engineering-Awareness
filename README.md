# Social-Engineering-Awareness
Analyze common social engineering scenarios and identify warning sings.


Overview :

This project focuses on understanding common social engineering attacks and identifying warning signs. It explains how attackers target human behavior through manipulation, trust, urgency, and deception.

 ---
 
Objective :

To learn how social engineering attacks work and how employees can identify and prevent them.

---

Common Social Engineering Scenarios :

- Phishing: Fake emails or messages used to steal information.
- Impersonation: Pretending to be a trusted person or organization.
- Pretexting: Creating a false story or identity to obtain information.
- Urgent Requests: Creating pressure to make the victim act quickly.
- Suspicious Requests: Asking for passwords, OTPs, confidential data, or unauthorized access.

---

Warning Signs :

- Unexpected requests for sensitive information
- Urgent or threatening messages
- Suspicious links or attachments
- Unknown or unusual email addresses
- Requests to bypass normal security procedures
- Someone pretending to be a trusted person

---

Prevention :

Employees can reduce the risk of social engineering by:

- Verifying the identity of the requester
- Never sharing passwords or OTPs
- Checking links and attachments carefully
- Following security procedures
- Using Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
- Reporting suspicious activity to the security or IT team

---

Interview Questions :

What is Social Engineering?

Social engineering is a technique ,
where attackers manipulate people into revealing sensitive information or performing unsafe actions.

What is Pretexting?

Pretexting is a technique,
where an attacker creates a believable false story or identity to obtain information or access.

How can employees prevent Social Engineering?

Employees can prevent social engineering by verifying requests, protecting sensitive information, 
following security policies, using MFA, and reporting suspicious activities.

---

Conclusion :

Social engineering attacks target human trust and behavior.
Awareness, verification, and following proper security practices can help reduce the risk of these attacks.
